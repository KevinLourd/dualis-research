Quality tests used for scoring (each 1 to 5): **Appropriate** (feels like institutional research, not fintech or a fund), **Distinctive** (no competitor or category cliché), **Memorable** (Haviv doodle test: can you draw it from memory after one look?), **Simple** (16 px, one colour), **Reversal** (white on #1A1640 and on photo), **Story** (one sentence, true to Thierry's method), **Timeless** (no trend: no gradients, no AI swirl, no chart glyph).

## 1. Brief

- **Client**: Dualis Research, independent equity research by Thierry Matin, founder and Lead Analyst.
- **What is special (butterfly, left wing)**: buy-side research, long AND short, small and mid caps in under-covered markets. Method: the price is a narrative, value is the business. Long when a bad narrative turns (price below fair value), short when a hyped one cracks (price above). "Play winnable games".
- **What the world needs (right wing)**: independent research that says "sell" as readily as "buy"; sell-side rarely does, small caps are rarely covered at all.
- **The one job of the mark**: be the thing a reader recognises in a Substack inbox, an X timeline and a report cover, and that looks as serious as a fund's letterhead. It must be ownable the way Citrini's glyph is.
- **Name**: Dualis, Latin "of two". The single richest asset we have. Latin also gives the inscriptional spelling DVALIS (V for U).
- **Constraints**: one colour (#2B2463, deep band #1A1640, white). No green/red. Vector geometry only, no AI imagery. Surfaces: 30 px header mark + wordmark, 16 px favicon, round avatar (Substack, X), white on photo (report covers), console sidebar.

## 2. Audience

Self-directed investors, analysts and small fund PMs who read Substack research (Citrini, Archetype, Liger Cub, DeepValue). They judge credibility in one glance and have seen every candlestick, bull and arrow. They respect restraint: the most trusted marks in their world (Lazard, Bridgewater, Citadel, Pershing Square) are plain type or one simple figure.

## 3. Positioning in one line

"The research that reads both sides of a stock: price is the story, value is the business."

**What the category does**: navy + serif caps (safe, forgettable), or literal illustrations (submarine, eye, arch, bars). **Gap**: a single, abstract, geometric figure with a true story, in one indigo. That is the Citrini lesson: the glyph is distinctive because it is not a picture of finance.

## 5. Symbol or wordmark? (Haviv's question)

A symbol is justified: Substack and X show only a round avatar at 40 to 80 px, the favicon is 16 px, and report covers need a small seal. A wordmark alone (rejected A) cannot do those jobs. So: symbol + a quiet wordmark; the symbol carries the identity.

## 6. Attribute words

Keep: two-sided, contrarian, rigorous, independent, calm, Latin, precise, patient.
Avoid: fast, tech, AI, trading-floor, bullish, gold, luxury, fund.

## 7. Concept territories

1. **The letter (T1)**. Own the D (and the d) the way Citrini owns its C. Story: the name itself is two-sided.
2. **The gap (T2)**. Price and value are the same company and are never level; the gap is the trade.
3. **The turn (T3)**. Peaks and troughs: short the peak, buy the trough; the narrative turns.
4. **The instrument (T4)**. Research as a tool that points the way: needle, lens, bracket.
5. **Latin heritage (T5)**. DVALIS, inscriptional Rome: gravity and permanence.
6. **Two readings (T6)**. One fact, read two ways (the market's story vs Thierry's view): figure-ground, convex/concave, ambigram.

## 8. Word-to-shape thumbnails (in words), with verdicts

T1 the letter
- a. Lowercase d + its 180° rotation share a stem: stem + two rings, rotationally symmetric, reads "d" either way up. **Keep (01)**.
- b. Two capital D's, one mirrored, bowls interlocked like chain links, over-under weave. **Keep (02)**. First try (D + D turned 180°, offset diagonally) produced crossings on the bowl/flat joints and read "Da": redrawn as a mirror pair whose crossings fall mid-arc.
- c. D drawn from one continuous line that loops into a mirrored lower bowl. Reject: reads as a "$" or a reversed S with a spine.
- d. D with a split / two-tone fill. Reject: that is the live stopgap (split D) and rejected C/D.
- e. "DR" ligature, shared stem. Reject: two letters in a 16 px box always blur; looks like a law firm.

T2 the gap
- f. A coin cut on its vertical axis, halves slid apart vertically (the halves are a D and a mirrored D). **Keep (03)**.
- g. A D whose stem (value) and bowl (price) have come apart and slipped. **Keep (04)**.
- h. Two horizontal bars offset ("not equal" without the slash). Reject: reads as a hamburger menu; too close to rejected B (two bars).
- i. Square split and slid. Reject: becomes two rectangles = rejected B.
- j. Bracket (fair value range) with a dot outside it. Reject: chart notation, explains rather than identifies (Rand).
- k. Eclipse: solid disc partly covering a ring. Reject: too near rejected F (two rings) and G (half-filled circle).

T3 the turn
- l. Sine wave with turning dots. Reject: that is rejected E and H.
- m. Two chevrons, peak above-left, trough below-right, never on one line. **Keep (06)**.
- n. U-turn arrow. Reject: UI "refresh" icon cliché.
- o. Up and down triangles side by side. Reject: data icons, and long/short belong to green/red data colours we may not use.
- p. Two quarter discs turned away from each other (Bauhaus). **Keep (09)** as the most "pure geometry" option, knowing it is the least ownable.

T4 the instrument
- q. Compass needle, one end solid, one end open, set at 45°. **Keep (07)**.
- r. Magnifying glass / loupe. Reject: research cliché.
- s. Eye / aperture. Reject: Archetype already owns an eye in this exact Substack niche.
- t. Corner brackets framing what others ignore (under-covered). Reject: reads as a screenshot / viewfinder icon.
- u. Scale / fulcrum (weighing value). Reject: reads as a law firm.

T5 Latin heritage
- v. DVALIS spelling: the V cut into a solid D as a notch, so the letter holds the trough. **Keep (08)**.
- w. Roman coin / seal roundel with DVALIS lettering. Reject as primary: lettering dies below 48 px; could be a secondary seal for print covers later.
- x. Roman numeral II in a circle. Reject: reads as a pause button.

T6 two readings
- y. A square cut by one arc: the same edge is convex on one side, concave on the other. **Keep (05)**.
- z. Rubin vase (two profiles / a vase). Reject: needs detail to be read at all, fails 16 px, and is a stock psychology image.
- aa. Necker cube / impossible figure. Reject: Penrose triangle is worn out, and "impossible" is the wrong message for research.
- bb. Möbius band (two sides that are one). Reject: hard to draw in one weight, used by many fintechs.
- cc. Die showing two pips ("winnable games"). Reject: gambling connotation is fatal for research.
- dd. Chess knight ("games"). Reject: finance cliché, illustrative.

Critical notes per mark:
- **01 Ambigram d**: strongest Citrini-like quality (a letter that is also a figure). Risk: at a glance it can read "dp" or a phi; at 16 px the two rings stay open with a 26/256 stroke (1.6 px). Turning it 180° gives the same mark: the story is demonstrable, not claimed.
- **02 Interlock**: very institutional, symmetrical, reads "DD" as a link. Weak test: the over-under gaps vanish at 16 px (they are 7/256 = 0.4 px); a 16 px favicon variant without gaps would be needed.
- **03 Slipped Coin**: the simplest and sturdiest; survives 16 px as a clean silhouette; the two halves ARE a D and a mirrored D. Weak test: distinctiveness is good but not unique (split circles exist in other sectors); the vertical slip is what makes it ours.
- **04 Slipped D**: best literal story, but at a glance it reads "IP" or "P". Kept to show the T2 letter route; would not go forward.
- **05 Two Readings**: elegant idea, but the shape reads as a generic corner tile; story needs explaining. Low distinctiveness.
- **06 Peak and Trough**: readable, but zigzags are crypto-adjacent and the angular rhythm feels like a trading app, not a research house.
- **07 Needle**: compass = consultancy cliché; the open half thins out at 16 px. Weakest.
- **08 DVALIS Notch**: the most "ours": nobody else can own the Latin V in Dualis. Solid shape, survives 16 px, reverses cleanly, the V doubles as the trough Thierry buys. Risk: the left sliver can read as a separate stroke ("ID"); the bridge under the V is kept at 36 units to hold it together.
- **09 Quarters**: purest geometry, best at 16 px, least ownable (Bauhaus quarter tiles are everywhere).

## 10. Refinement notes (construction and optics)

Grid: 256 units, live area 20 to 236 (a 20-unit margin so the avatar crop and 16 px rasterisation do not touch the edge). Stroke logic: monoline marks use 19 to 30 units (01: 26, 02: 20 × 1.3 scale = 26, 06: 30) so every stroke is at least 1.5 px at 16 px; solid marks keep every gap at 14 to 22 units (≥ 0.9 px at 16 px).

- **01**: one vertical stem x=128 from y=23 to y=233 (butt caps), rings r=46 (centre line) with stroke 26, centres (82,174) and (174,82): the ring centre line passes through the stem centre, so each bowl merges into the stem like a real d. Exactly 180° rotationally symmetric about (128,128).
- **02**: D and mirror D, bowl radius 66, flats 22, bowl centres 76 apart (90 and 166) so the two crossings fall on x=128 mid-arc, far from the joints. Weave: D1 over at the top crossing, D2 over at the bottom (mask gap 7 each side). The first construction (D + D turned 180°, diagonal offset) put both crossings on the bowl/flat joints: redrawn.
- **03**: disc r=90 cut on x=128, gap 14, left half raised 22, right half lowered 22 (equal and opposite, so the mark stays centred on (128,128)).
- **04**: stem 34 × 160, bowl stroke 34 (outer radius 80), gap 18, bowl raised 28; whole figure centred on its bounding box.
- **05**: square 36 to 220; arc centred on the bottom-left corner, radius 150, cut 16 wide; convex quarter disc r=142, concave remainder outside r=158.
- **06**: two 90° chevrons, stroke 30, miter joins; peak apex (94,58), trough apex (162,198); arms end on a shared diagonal so the two shapes read as one broken diamond.
- **07**: rhombus 228 long, 116 wide, turned 45° (pointing north-east); north half solid, south half an inside stroke of 11 clipped to its triangle so the outline never grows outside the silhouette.
- **08**: solid D: stem x=40, flat to 128, bowl r=96 centred (128,128); V cut from the top edge, apex (108,188), opening 84 at the top; bridge below the apex 36 units = close to the stem's width at the top (≈30) so weights match.
- **09**: two quarter discs r=100 sharing the centre, each pulled 7 units outward along the diagonal so they never touch at 16 px.

Optical corrections applied in lockups (`OPT` in `gen.py`): square or bounding-box-filling marks look larger than round ones at the same box, so 05 is shown at 86 %, 02 and 08 at 92 %, 04 at 95 %. Round marks (03, 09) overshoot the box edge by the classic ~2 to 3 % in their masters (r=90 and 100 on a 216 live area vs. the square at 184).

## 11. Wordmark typography

Kept: **Newsreader 500, all caps, +7 % tracking** for DUALIS, **IBM Plex Sans 500, +42 % tracking, at about one third the cap height** for RESEARCH, set under it. Why: Newsreader is already the site's reading face (consistency across site, Substack, reports), its caps have a calm bookish contrast that balances geometric marks (a geometric sans beside a geometric mark would look like a startup), and both are free (SIL OFL). Alternative considered: Cormorant Garamond caps (free, Google Fonts) for a more Roman, inscriptional feel that would suit 08; rejected for now because its thin hairlines break at 14 px header sizes. Before final, the DUALIS wordmark should be outlined and kerned by hand (the A-L and L-I pairs open up in caps).

## 12. Lockups

1. Horizontal (header, 30 to 40 px mark): mark left, two-line wordmark right, gap = 0.3 × mark height, wordmark cap height ≈ 0.45 × mark height, centred on the mark's optical middle.
2. Mark alone: avatar, favicon, console sidebar collapsed, report-cover seal.
3. Stacked (covers, print): mark above, DUALIS, RESEARCH (not drawn this round).

## 13. Colour and reversal

One colour only: #2B2463 on white; white on #1A1640 and on photos (report covers). The marks are all single-colour shapes with no internal tone, so reversal is a straight swap (checked on the board, column 2). No gradient, no second brand colour, never green/red.

On the board, column 3: true 16 px render (1× pixels), 32 px, 16 px on a light browser-tab chip, and the round avatar (84 px, mark at 60 %). | **Coins and medals** | Profile in relief, beaded rim, legend ring, exergue date | A closed, centred composition with a hierarchy of rim, legend and portrait; the Roman as carried Janus from 240 BC (moneymuseum.com; CoinWeek; NGC) |
| **Roman inscriptional capitals (Trajan)** | Height : widest : narrowest ≈ 10 : 1 : ½; serif born from the flat brush (Catich) | Proportion and stress, not decoration, make them noble (Trajan Pro 3 specimen; Wikipedia "Edward Catich"; Paul Shaw) |
| **Bookplates, ex libris; private-bank crests** | Framed devices with a motto | A frame + an emblem + a word = ownership and lineage |
| **Swiss modernist monograms** | Two letters joined on a grid | One construction rule applied without exception |

**What I take from it (5 lines):**
1. Expensive marks borrow an OLD meaning (Rothschild arrows, Janus on the as, Hindenburg's airship, banknote lace) rather than invent a new metaphor; the story is checkable.
2. Craft shows in LINE HIERARCHY: rim, rule, beads, legend, field hatch, subject, each at its own weight; one weight everywhere reads cheap.
3. Engraving is the native language of money; a few hundred fine lines say "official" before anything is read.
4. The strongest institutions use restraint at small size: the hero can be intricate, the 16 px form must be one silhouette; that is a responsive pair, not one compromise mark.
5. Real letterforms (Roman capitals, a true D with serifs) beat geometric pseudo-letters; Citrini's glyph works because its geometry is exact, not because it is simple.

## R2.3 Responsive identity rule

Each proposal = DETAILED (hero: website header at 120 px+, report covers, about page; fine lines from 0.5 to 4.5 units, many strokes allowed) + SMALL (16-48 px, avatar, favicon, console sidebar): drawn again from the same idea, never a scaled-down copy; minimum stroke 9 units (12+ wherever the silhouette depends on it, about 0.75 px at 16 px), minimum gap 7 units.

## R2.4 Craft bar applied

- **Line hierarchy** (detailed versions): rim 3.4-4.5 → primary rule 1.0-1.1 → beads 1.25-1.7 → field hatch 0.5 → subject lines 1.1-1.3 → subject outline 3.2. No two layers share a weight.

## R2.5 Wordmark

Switched from Newsreader to **Cinzel** (Google Fonts, SIL OFL), outlined to paths (`r2-wordmark.svg`): DUALIS at 500, +16 % tracking; a 52-unit hairline rule; RESEARCH at 500, +55 % tracking, one third the size. Why: every round-2 proposal is Roman / numismatic / engraved, and Cinzel is drawn from first-century Roman capitals (Trajan proportions), so the mark and the name speak one language. Kept the U (DUALIS, the brand name) in the wordmark; the V spelling lives only in the Latin legend and the DV monogram. Rejected: Cormorant SC (beautiful, but hairlines break at header size), EB Garamond SC (bookish, not monumental), Trajan (paid). Before final: hand-kern A-L and L-I, and give the U its inscriptional V-like taper.

## R2.7 Top 3

1. **03 Solomon's Knot**: a real 2,000-year-old Roman motif, so the story is checkable; it is literally "two" and "inseparable" (price and value, long and short). Best responsive pair: banded engraving at hero size, a bold weave at 16 px. No competitor or fintech owns it.
2. **01 Janus**: the most out-of-the-box and the most memorable story (Janus looks both ways, and he was ON Roman money). It reads as a coin at once. Needs a professional engraver's pass on the profile before it can ship; the small silhouette is its weak point.
3. **08 The Turn**: the Citrini-grade answer. One line, two exactly tangent circles, ending at the centre of the D. It is the cleanest at 16 px and the most ownable letter. It is the least "engraved", so pair it with the Cinzel wordmark and a guilloche band on report covers.

Honourable mention: 05 Tabula DV, if the owner prefers a pure-typographic Roman direction.
