## 1. The business

Harlow makes industrial fasteners for truck and machinery makers. Revenue was $760M in 2025, 70% from ten large customers. (EXAMPLE figures.)

Margins fell from 14.8% in 2019 to 9.6% in 2021 when steel costs spiked. Since then they have climbed every year, to 12.4% in 2025.

The stock trades at 6.8x 2025 EBITDA, close to its lowest level in ten years.

## 2. Our view

### Driver 1: Pricing sticks
Harlow raised prices three times since 2022 and kept every top-ten customer. Steel costs are back near 2019 levels, so the gap now flows to margin.

### Driver 2: The new plant
The plant opens in Q2 2027. Management guides to 12% lower unit costs on the lines it replaces.

### Driver 3: Cash goes back to holders
Free cash flow covers a 4% yearly buyback with net debt flat at $120M.

## 3. Bull vs Bear

[IMAGE: images/02-bull-vs-bear.png | caption: none]

We side with the bull case on margins and stay cautious on volumes. The base case only needs margins back to 15%, below the 2019 peak.

## 4. Catalysts

- **Q1 2027**: full-year 2026 results, first guide that includes the new plant.
- **Q2 2027**: plant opening and first unit-cost data.
- **H2 2027**: debt refinancing, which settles the interest-cost risk.

## 5. What if we're wrong

Volumes are the main risk. A truck downturn in 2027 would hit revenue before the plant helps.

Customer concentration is the second. Losing one top-ten customer would cut revenue by about 6%.

**Kill criteria.** We exit if volumes fall for two quarters in a row, or if the 2027 guide implies margins below 12%.
